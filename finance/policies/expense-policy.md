# Expense Policy - Graph Paper LLC

Guidelines for business expenses and reimbursements for a single-member LLC.

## General Principles

1. **Ordinary and Necessary:** Expense must be common and helpful for your business
2. **Business Purpose:** Must have legitimate business reason
3. **Documented:** Keep receipts and document purpose
4. **Reasonable:** Amount must be reasonable for the expense type

---

## Approved Expense Categories

### Technology & Software
**What's Covered:**
- Development tools and IDEs
- SaaS subscriptions (productivity, business tools)
- Cloud hosting and infrastructure
- Domain names and SSL certificates
- Software licenses

**Examples:**
- ✅ GitHub subscription
- ✅ AWS/cloud hosting
- ✅ Adobe Creative Cloud (if used for business)
- ✅ Google Workspace
- ❌ Netflix (personal entertainment)

**Documentation:** Receipt showing business name or purpose

---

### Equipment & Supplies
**What's Covered:**
- Computers, monitors, peripherals
- Office furniture (desk, chair)
- Office supplies
- Tools and equipment for business use

**Rules:**
- Items >$2,500: May need to depreciate over time
- Items <$2,500: Can expense immediately (Section 179)
- Home use items: Only business-use percentage deductible

**Examples:**
- ✅ Laptop for development work
- ✅ External monitor
- ✅ Office chair and desk
- ✅ Printer and supplies
- ⚠️ TV (only if used exclusively for business presentations)

**Documentation:** Receipt + note on business use

---

### Home Office
**Two Methods:**

**Simplified Method:**
- $5 per square foot
- Maximum 300 square feet
- No depreciation tracking

**Actual Expense Method:**
- Calculate % of home used for business
- Deduct that % of: mortgage/rent, utilities, insurance, repairs
- More complex but may yield larger deduction

**Requirements:**
- Regular and exclusive use for business
- Principal place of business

**Documentation:**
- Measurements and floor plan
- Photos of dedicated workspace
- Utility bills, mortgage/rent statements

### Transportation & Travel

**Vehicle Expenses - Two Methods:**

**Standard Mileage Rate:**
- Track miles driven for business
- Multiply by IRS standard rate (65.5¢/mile for 2023)
- Simpler, no receipt tracking

**Actual Expense Method:**
- Track % of business use
- Deduct that % of: gas, maintenance, insurance, depreciation
- More complex, need all receipts

**Mileage Log Must Include:**
- Date
- Starting location
- Destination
- Business purpose
- Miles driven
- Odometer readings

**Travel Expenses:**
- Airfare, lodging, meals (50% deductible)
- Ground transportation
- Conference fees

**Examples:**
- ✅ Drive to client meeting
- ✅ Trip to business conference
- ✅ Drive to buy inventory for Ladybug Gifts
- ❌ Regular commute to office (not deductible)

**Documentation:** Mileage log app or spreadsheet, receipts for travel expenses

### Meals & Entertainment

**Business Meals: 50% Deductible**
- Meals with clients, potential clients, or business associates
- Meals during business travel
- Business discussion must occur

**Not Deductible:**
- Lavish or extravagant meals
- Personal meals
- Meal with spouse (unless spouse is employee)

**Documentation Required:**
- Receipt
- Who attended
- Business purpose/topic discussed
- Date and location

**Example Note:** "Lunch with [Client Name] to discuss [Project/Proposal] - $45"

## Expense Documentation Requirements

### Minimum Required for All Expenses
1. **Receipt or invoice**
2. **Date of expense**
3. **Amount paid**
4. **Vendor/payee**
5. **Business purpose** (brief note)

### Best Practices
- Take photo of receipt immediately
- Store digitally (cloud backup)
- Note business purpose on receipt or in system
- Reconcile credit card statements monthly
- Keep originals for 7 years

### Tools
- Receipt scanning apps (Expensify, Receipt Bank, etc.)
- Accounting software (QuickBooks, Wave, Xero)
- Cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox)

---

## Owner's Draw vs. Business Expense

### Business Expense (Deductible)
- Reduces business income
- Lowers your tax liability
- Must have business purpose
- Properly documented

### Owner's Draw (Not Deductible)
- Personal money withdrawn from business
- Does NOT reduce taxable income
- Reduces business equity
- No tax benefit

**Examples:**
- ❌ Groceries for personal use (even if bought on business card)
- ❌ Personal clothing (unless required uniform)
- ❌ Personal entertainment
- ❌ Family vacation (unless legitimate business purpose)

---

## Mixed-Use Expenses

Some expenses have both personal and business use:

**Phone & Internet:**
- Calculate % used for business
- Only deduct business portion
- Keep records of calculation

**Vehicle:**
- Track business miles vs. total miles
- Only business use % is deductible

**Computer:**
- If used for both, estimate business use %
- 100% business use requires exclusive business use

**Home:**
- Home office deduction only for space used exclusively for business

## Expense Reimbursement Process

### For Single-Member LLC (You)
1. Pay expense from personal funds or business credit card
2. Document expense with receipt and purpose
3. Enter in accounting system
4. If paid personally: Reimburse yourself via check/transfer from business account
5. Categorize properly for tax purposes

### Track Two Ways
**Business Account → Direct Expense:**
- Easier, cleaner
- Expense paid directly from business funds

**Personal Account → Reimbursement:**
- You pay personally
- Business reimburses you
- Must document as reimbursement (not income)

## Red Flags to Avoid

### IRS Audit Triggers
- ❌ Claiming 100% vehicle use for business (unlikely)
- ❌ Excessive meal and entertainment expenses
- ❌ Large home office deduction relative to income
- ❌ Round numbers (looks estimated, not actual)
- ❌ No documentation or receipts

### Common Mistakes
- Mixing personal and business expenses
- No business purpose documentation
- Missing receipts
- Claiming personal expenses as business
- Not tracking mileage contemporaneously

---

## Monthly Expense Review

**By 5th of Each Month:**
1. Download bank and credit card statements
2. Categorize all transactions
3. Ensure receipts are captured
4. Document business purpose for unclear items
5. Reconcile accounts in accounting software
6. Review for any personal expenses to reclassify

---

## Year-End Expense Planning

**November-December:**
- Review YTD income and expenses
- Identify any needed equipment/supplies purchases
- Maximize deductible expenses if profitable year
- Prepay January expenses in December (if cash basis)
- Coordinate with CPA on tax strategy
